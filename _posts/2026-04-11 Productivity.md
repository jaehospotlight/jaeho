When a company is at 30 people, **what is the most productive usage of an operators time?** 

I'd note: 
* Education
* Hiring Good People
* Aligning & Setting KPI (*Not here yet)

The hardest part of education is the linear aspect of it. 

1 on 1 education has the highest ROI (what does corporate statistic say about this), but is near impossible, "Though Bloom concluded that one-to-one tutoring is "too costly for most societies to bear on a large scale", Bloom conjectured that a combination of two or three altered variables may result in a similar performance improvement."

The other part is follow up/iteration. Ensuring they are doing everything. **Especially in a fast paced environment**, I've realized non-experienced hires tend to 'miss things' and **as part of their manager** it's your job to catch that. 

**I think the ideal onboarding is a 2 week to learn everything.** 
**14 day:** Learn Everything
**30 day:** Shadow Senior Account Manager & Preps Material for them
**60 day:** Runs Through Agenda with SAM Support. Gets an operations manager. 
**90 day:** Runs Through Brand independently. Get an operations & creator manager. 

**14 Day Format:** 
**Weekly Goal:**
**Today's Output:** [Link or attach the actual deliverable — the email, the deck, the brief. Not a description of what they did, but the thing itself.]
**Checklist Completed?** Yes / No — if no, what was missed and why?
**Where I Got Stuck:** [Forces them to surface confusion before it becomes a mistake. Most new hires won't volunteer this unless prompted.]
**Tomorrow's Top 3:** [What they think their priorities are, in order]

**Task Tracking:** 
* Give it agenda
* Verify Agenda - is it good. 
* Create tasks on Clickup 
* Verifies if people are doing their job.
* Based on the affect, can Create Ad-Hoc Tasks
* Send Reminders for Tasks
