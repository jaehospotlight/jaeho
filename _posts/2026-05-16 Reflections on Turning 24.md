

**Personal**

For the past 3 years (since the start of Spotlight), I've only focused on my work. Originally, it was necessary. If not my co-founder and I, no one else could do the work. Additionally, it was the perfect escape from my prior relationship. 

But Spotlight today is now different-we have 30+ people. Yet, my lifestyle hasn't changed. What was once a necessity has simply turned into an 'escape'.

When friendships started deteriorating, it was the perfect 'excuse'. I had to consider the priorities of my company-the livelihood of 20 employees vs a singular friend. If not the false moral imperative, it was the wavering boundaries of my professional and personal life, where seeing 'employees come and go', made me too willing to let go of 'friendships'. 

It was only on my 24th birthday did I realize, how 'little' effort I put, relative to my friends. To all the friends-especially the ones I ghosted when things got hard-thank you for reaching out again. To my friends who hosted the surprise birthday party today, thank you for **reminding me, there are things more important than work**

To existent/future founds reading this - I don't know if this advice will resonate. Brian Chesky from AirBnb once said the below and I didn't listen. I hope you will. 

"We go public, we have a hundred billion dollar valuation. It's one of the best days of my life. The next day, I go on a Zoom meeting, and it was like it never happened." "It became like the saddest day of my life. Because I realized, I got all this adulation, and I don't feel any different."

**Unsolicited Advice:** 

* Relationships only get harder to form. Like a client, make sure to prevent churn and fight to maintain. 
* Growth isn't restricted to professional things. There's also a y axis and a slope for health & relationships (personal). 
* Reach Out. Even at Spotlight's size (X0,000,000) I read nearly all emails.   

**Professional**

Spotlight has grown a long way since Sam & I first started, starting with NoCapping. As we've grown to a team of 30 a fundamental question has been, 'what's next'. Do we remain an agency? Do we branch off to a brand? 

Additionally, management of the company has changed. There's now a 'formal' management structure. From having a general manager to 'senior' level staff. 

Truthfully, I wish the company was not so 'political' and people could simply trust us. **There is simply so much to do.** 

With AI, it seem like we can now make more mistakes on the 'what' as the cost of prototyping cheapens, but less on the 'how', and most definitely not on the 'who'. 

**Some General Thoughts:**
* Incentives vs TOF: To win at anything, you need to master incentives. While you figure out incentives though (Spotlight didn't have commission structures up until last quarter) as long as your TOF is good you're ok. But the cost is tribal knowledge. 
* Agencies: Agencies fundamentally are bad value - No WLB and low pay. We must envision a POD structure/path where the following can truly be true https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/ 
* The consumer world is increasingly going offline, which disrupts the AI thesis.
